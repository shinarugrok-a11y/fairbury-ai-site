The five habits

Every trick in the book loses to the same few moves. Learn these and you're covered for most of what's out there.

1

Pause. Hang up first. Decide later.

The single most useful habit there is. Anyone who pressures you to decide right now is handing you the tell. A real office will still be there tomorrow.

2

Judge the request, not the writing.

Scam letters and messages look real now. Don't grade the spelling. Ask what they want you to do, and whether a real office would ask for it that way.

3

Set a family safe word.

Pick one word everyone in your house knows. A real caller in a real emergency knows it. A stranger with a cloned voice doesn't.

4

Check the letter yourself.

A scam letter hands you their phone number. Ignore it. Look up the office's real number yourself and call that one.

5

If you'd shred it, don't type it.

Social Security numbers, account and card numbers, passwords, medical details. Chatbots and search boxes can keep what you type. The paper rule works: shred-worthy means don't type it.

Mail red flags

Paper scams run the same playbook as phone scams. Watch for these four.

  • A prize you never entered, or a threat about money you don't remember owing.
  • Payment by gift card, wire transfer, or crypto. Real offices don't take gift cards.
  • "You have to decide right now." Pressure is the tell, on paper too.
  • A phone number printed on the letter, with instructions to call it. Look up the real number yourself.

What never goes into AI or search

Questions are fine. Private facts are not.

  • Full Social Security number
  • Bank account numbers, card numbers, PINs and passwords
  • Medical details, yours or anyone else's
  • Other people's private information

If you'd shred the paper version, don't type it.

If it already happened

Fast, calm, in this order.

1

Tell someone you trust.

Shame keeps people quiet, and scammers count on that. Talking about it protects the next person.

2

If money moved, call your bank right now.

Use the number on your card, not the number they gave you.

3

Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

It takes a few minutes, and it helps investigators see the pattern.

Fair questions

The ones people actually ask.

Will fAIrbury Academy ever ask for my password or account number?

No. We never need it, and we will never ask by phone, text, or email. If someone claiming to be us asks, hang up and tell us through the form below.

Is it safe to use AI at all?

For learning, yes. The rule is one sentence: never type private information into it. Questions are fine. Your Social Security number is not.

I got a scary call. What do I do first?

Hang up. That is step one, and it is enough. Then talk to someone you trust before you do anything else.

Can I bring a suspicious letter to class?

Yes. Bring it to a free session and we will read it together. Do not call the number printed on it first.
